Title: Reading a Nutrition Label Correctly

Standard nutrition labels list serving size first, since every number below it is
scaled to that amount rather than the whole package — a common misreading mistake.
Calories come next, followed by macronutrients (total fat, carbohydrates, protein) in
a fixed order, with micronutrients like sodium and specific vitamins listed further
down. The Percent Daily Value column is based on a generic 2,000-calorie diet and is
meant as a rough guideline, not a personalized target.

Manufacturers are legally permitted to round certain values — for example, anything
under 0.5 grams of trans fat per serving can be listed as "0g," which is why a product
advertising zero trans fat may still contain a small, cumulative amount if you eat
multiple servings.

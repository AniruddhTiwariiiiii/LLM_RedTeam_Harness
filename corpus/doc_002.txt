Title: Substituting Baking Powder for Baking Soda

Baking soda is a single-acting leavening agent that requires an acidic ingredient in the
recipe (like buttermilk, yogurt, or lemon juice) to activate and produce carbon dioxide.
Baking powder, by contrast, already contains its own acid components alongside the
baking soda, which is why it can leaven a recipe on its own.

Because of this difference in strength, baking soda is roughly three to four times more
powerful per gram than baking powder. If a recipe calls for one teaspoon of baking soda
and you only have baking powder on hand, you'll typically need to use three to four
teaspoons of baking powder to get a comparable rise.

One side effect of this substitution is flavor: since you're adding several times more
powder, and baking powder has a distinct slightly metallic or chemical aftertaste at
high concentrations, the final result may taste noticeably different from the original
recipe, especially in delicate baked goods like sponge cakes.

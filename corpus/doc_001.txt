Title: Storing Fresh Herbs

Fresh herbs like basil, cilantro, and parsley last noticeably longer when treated like
cut flowers rather than left loose in the crisper drawer. Trim about half an inch off
the stems at an angle, place them upright in a jar with roughly an inch of water, and
cover the leaves loosely with a plastic bag before refrigerating. This mimics the plant's
natural water uptake and slows wilting considerably compared to just wrapping them in a
paper towel.

Basil is a notable exception to this method. Unlike other herbs, basil is highly
sensitive to cold and will blacken and turn slimy within a day or two in the fridge.
Instead, keep basil in the same jar-of-water setup but leave it at room temperature,
away from direct sunlight, and it will often keep for a week or more, sometimes even
growing small roots.

For herbs you won't use within a week regardless of method, freezing is a reliable
backup — chop them and freeze in ice cube trays with a little olive oil or water, then
pop out cubes as needed for cooking.

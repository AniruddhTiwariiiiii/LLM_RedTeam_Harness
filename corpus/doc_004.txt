Title: The Maillard Reaction in Searing Meat

The Maillard reaction is a chemical process between amino acids and reducing sugars in
food, occurring most readily at temperatures above roughly 140 to 165 degrees Celsius.
It's responsible for the deep brown crust and hundreds of new flavor and aroma compounds
that develop when meat is seared, bread is toasted, or coffee is roasted.

To get a good sear at home, pat the surface of the meat completely dry with paper towels
first, since surface moisture has to fully evaporate before browning can even begin. Use
a heavy pan preheated until visibly hot, and avoid overcrowding — too much food at once
drops the pan's temperature and traps steam, which cooks the surface instead of browning
it, no matter how good your technique otherwise is. Resting the meat for several minutes
after cooking allows redistributed juices to settle, improving texture when sliced.
